# Saint-Pierre ruins

## Overview

Saint-Pierre, on the north-west coast of Martinique in the Caribbean, was once the island's capital and its cultural and economic heart, known as the "Paris of the Caribbean". On the morning of 8 May 1902, the nearby volcano Mount Pelée erupted, and a cloud of superheated gas and ash swept down onto the town, destroying it within minutes and killing almost all of its roughly 28,000 inhabitants. One of the very few survivors was a prisoner, Ludger Sylbaris, protected by the thick walls of his cell, who later toured with a circus. The town was gradually rebuilt on a smaller scale, but many ruins were left standing among the new houses, including the theatre, the prison cell and the old warehouses of the Figuier district by the sea. Ships sunk during the eruption lie in the bay and are popular with divers. A memorial museum tells the story of the disaster.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Martinique Aimé Césaire International Airport (FDF), about 40 km, allow an hour.
- **Bus:** shared taxis run from Fort-de-France to Saint-Pierre.

## Visiting

The ruins in the town are freely accessible, and the memorial museum charges an entry fee. Mount Pelée is still active and closely monitored; hiking trails lead up the volcano. Saint-Pierre is a place where thousands died, so visit respectfully.
