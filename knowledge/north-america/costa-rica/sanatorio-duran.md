# Sanatorio Durán

## Overview

The Sanatorio Durán lies on the slopes of the Irazú volcano, about 2,300 metres above sea level, north of the city of Cartago in Costa Rica. It was founded in 1918 by the doctor and politician Carlos Durán Cartín as a sanatorium for tuberculosis patients, who were sent up to the cool, clean mountain air to recover. Over the years the complex grew into a large hospital with wards, a chapel and staff housing, treating hundreds of patients, many of them children. When antibiotics made sanatoriums unnecessary, the hospital closed in the 1970s, and after a period of other uses the buildings were abandoned. Their peeling paint, long corridors and graffiti have given the sanatorium a reputation as one of the most haunted places in Costa Rica, and it has appeared on television shows about the paranormal. Today the site is open to visitors for a small fee, with mist often drifting through the ruins.

## Getting there

- **Car or taxi:** from Juan Santamaría International Airport (SJO), about 45 km, allow 1¼ hours via Cartago on the road towards the Irazú volcano.

## Visiting

The site is open during the day with a small entry fee. The buildings are unstable, so stay out of fenced areas. It is often cold and foggy at this altitude. A visit combines well with Irazú Volcano National Park further up the road.
