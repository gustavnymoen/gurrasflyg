# Salton Sea and Bombay Beach

## Overview

The Salton Sea is a large, salty lake in the desert of southern California, created by accident in 1905 when the Colorado River broke through irrigation canals and flooded a dry basin for nearly two years. In the 1950s and 1960s it became a fashionable resort area, with marinas, yacht clubs and holiday towns that drew huge numbers of visitors. But with no outlet, the lake grew saltier and was polluted by farm run-off, floods damaged shoreline buildings, and mass fish deaths left beaches covered in bones. Most visitors stopped coming, and towns such as Bombay Beach were left half empty, with ruined trailers and houses sinking into the mud. In recent years artists have moved to Bombay Beach, creating installations among the ruins and holding an annual art event, while the lake keeps shrinking and exposing dusty shores.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Palm Springs International Airport (PSP), about 90 km, allow 1¼ hours. There is no practical public transport.

## Visiting

The shore and the art installations are freely accessible. Summer temperatures can exceed 45 °C, so winter and spring are the best times to visit. The lake sometimes smells strongly. In Bombay Beach, respect residents' property.
