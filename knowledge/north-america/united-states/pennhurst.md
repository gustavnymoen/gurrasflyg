# Pennhurst State School and Hospital

## Overview

Pennhurst State School and Hospital, near Spring City in south-eastern Pennsylvania, opened in 1908 as an institution for people with intellectual and physical disabilities. Built as a campus of brick buildings on a hillside, it grew far beyond its intended size and by the 1960s housed nearly 3,000 people in overcrowded wards. Conditions were often appalling, with widespread reports of neglect and abuse. In 1968 a television report, "Suffer the Little Children", exposed the conditions to the public, and a federal lawsuit filed in 1974 led to court rulings that helped establish the right of disabled people to live in their communities rather than in institutions. Pennhurst closed in 1987. Its empty buildings decayed for years, and part of the campus is now run as a seasonal haunted attraction, a use that disability advocates have criticised. A memorial and preservation group works to keep the real history alive.

## Visiting

The site is private property. It can only be visited during the seasonal attraction in the autumn or on occasional history tours; at other times, do not enter. Thousands of people lived and suffered here, which is worth keeping in mind during a visit.
