# Sloss Furnaces

## Overview

Sloss Furnaces is a former blast-furnace plant on the edge of downtown Birmingham, Alabama, a city that grew up around iron and steel. The company was founded in 1881 by James Withers Sloss, and its furnaces produced pig iron for about ninety years, until 1971. Much of the hard and dangerous work was done by Black workers, often for low wages and under segregation, and the museum today tells their story along with that of the industry. After it closed, the plant was saved from demolition and became a National Historic Landmark, one of very few blast-furnace plants in the United States preserved as a historic site. Visitors can walk among the towering furnaces, boilers, stoves and pipes. Sloss is also a centre for metal arts, with workshops and public iron pours, and it hosts concerts, festivals and a popular Halloween haunted house.

## Getting there

- **Taxi:** from Birmingham-Shuttlesworth International Airport (BHM), about 5 km, allow 10 minutes.

## Visiting

The grounds are open to visitors several days a week; check the opening days. Guided tours are available. It can be very hot in summer, and there is little shade among the furnaces.
