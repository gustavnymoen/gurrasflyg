# Kennecott

## Overview

Kennecott is a former copper-mining camp deep inside Wrangell-St. Elias, the largest national park in the United States, in south-eastern Alaska. Between 1911 and 1938 the Kennecott Copper Corporation mined some of the richest copper ore ever found in the mountains above the Kennicott Glacier and shipped it to the coast on its own railway. A company town grew up around a towering, red-painted concentration mill, with a hospital, a school, a power plant and bunkhouses for hundreds of workers. When the ore ran out, the company left almost overnight, and the buildings were abandoned. The National Park Service bought the site in 1998 and has been stabilising and restoring it ever since. Today the red buildings stand out against the white and grey of the glacier, and visitors can tour the fourteen-storey mill and hike to the old mines.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Ted Stevens Anchorage International Airport (ANC), about 500 km, allow seven to eight hours. The last 95 km follow the unpaved McCarthy Road to a footbridge, where cars must be left.
- **Walk or shuttle:** cross the footbridge on foot; a shuttle covers the remaining 8 km to Kennecott.
- Small air-taxi companies also fly to the airstrip at McCarthy.

## Visiting

Guided tours of the mill run in summer, from about late May to early September; the grounds are free to walk around, and there is a park visitor centre. Services are very limited. This is bear country, so follow the park's safety advice.
