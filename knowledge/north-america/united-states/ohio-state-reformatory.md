# Ohio State Reformatory

## Overview

The Ohio State Reformatory in Mansfield, Ohio, was built between 1886 and 1910 as a reformatory for young first-time offenders. Its massive administration building mixes several architectural styles, from Romanesque to Gothic, and looks more like a castle than a prison. Behind it stands one of the largest free-standing steel cellblocks in the world, six tiers high. The reformatory later became an ordinary prison, and conditions deteriorated until it closed in 1990 after a court ruling on overcrowding. Shortly afterwards it became famous as the main filming location of The Shawshank Redemption, released in 1994, and it has since appeared in many other films and music videos. Today a preservation society runs the building as a museum, with self-guided and guided tours of the cellblocks, the warden's quarters and the chapel, as well as ghost hunts at night.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Cleveland Hopkins International Airport (CLE), about 115 km, allow 1¼ hours. There is no practical public transport.

## Visiting

The reformatory is open most days with an entry fee. Self-guided tours, guided tours and evening ghost walks are available. Parts of the building are unheated, so dress warmly in winter.
