# Bodie

## Overview

Bodie is a former gold-mining town high in the hills east of the Sierra Nevada in California, close to the Nevada border. Gold was found here in 1859, and after a rich discovery in the late 1870s the town boomed to several thousand people, with dozens of saloons, a Chinatown, churches and a reputation for lawlessness and violence. As the mines declined in the early twentieth century, people drifted away, and by the 1940s Bodie was almost deserted. In 1962 it became Bodie State Historic Park. Around a hundred buildings still stand, kept in a state of "arrested decay": they are repaired just enough to stop them from collapsing, but not restored. Through dusty windows, visitors can see furniture, tools, shelves of goods and schoolbooks left behind, which makes Bodie one of the most authentic ghost towns in the American West.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Reno-Tahoe International Airport (RNO), about 190 km, allow 2½–3 hours via US Highway 395 and Bridgeport. The last few kilometres are on an unpaved road. There is no public transport.

## Visiting

The park is open all year with an entry fee, but snow can close the road in winter. No food or water is sold, so bring your own. At about 2,500 metres, the weather can be extreme. Local legend says that anyone who takes something from Bodie will have bad luck, and taking anything is forbidden anyway.
