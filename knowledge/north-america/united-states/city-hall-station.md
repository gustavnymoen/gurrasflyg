# City Hall Station

## Overview

City Hall station was the southern terminus of New York City's first subway line, which opened on 27 October 1904. Designed as the showpiece of the new system, it was built on a tight curve beneath City Hall Park, with elegant arched ceilings covered in tiles, brass chandeliers and skylights of coloured glass. As trains grew longer, the curved platform became too short and too dangerous for the new cars, and the station, which had never been busy, closed in 1945. It has been preserved almost unchanged ever since, hidden beneath one of the busiest parts of Manhattan. The New York Transit Museum occasionally runs tours for its members, and anyone can catch a glimpse of the station by staying on board a downtown 6 train as it turns around through the loop after its last stop at Brooklyn Bridge-City Hall.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** LaGuardia Airport (LGA) is about 15 km away; John F. Kennedy International (JFK) is the main international airport.
- **Metro:** from LaGuardia, take a bus to the subway, then the 4, 5 or 6 train to Brooklyn Bridge-City Hall, about an hour in total.

## Visiting

The station can only be entered on New York Transit Museum tours, which are for members and sell out quickly. To glimpse it from the train, stay on a downtown 6 train after the last stop at Brooklyn Bridge-City Hall: it loops through the old station and returns uptown.
