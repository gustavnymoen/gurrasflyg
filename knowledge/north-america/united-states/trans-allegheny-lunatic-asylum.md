# Trans-Allegheny Lunatic Asylum

## Overview

The Trans-Allegheny Lunatic Asylum in Weston, West Virginia, is one of the largest hand-cut sandstone buildings in North America. It was built between 1858 and 1881 following the ideas of the psychiatrist Thomas Kirkbride, who believed that a calm, spacious building with plenty of light and fresh air could help patients recover. Designed for about 250 patients, it held more than 2,000 by the 1950s, and overcrowding, poor conditions and harsh treatments replaced the original ideals. The hospital, later known as Weston State Hospital, closed in 1994. Its long main building, with a clock tower at the centre and wings stretching out on both sides, stood empty for years. Since the late 2000s private owners have opened it for tours that explain the history of mental health care, along with popular paranormal tours at night.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** the nearest airport is North Central West Virginia (CKB), about 45 km away. Pittsburgh International (PIT), about 200 km away, has many more flights.
- **Car:** about 40 minutes from CKB, or 2½ hours from Pittsburgh.

## Visiting

Visits are on guided tours only, ranging from history tours to night-time paranormal tours; book ahead. Some tours are long and involve many stairs. Remember that this was a hospital where many people suffered.
