# Garnet

## Overview

Garnet is a ghost town in the Garnet Range of western Montana, about 1,800 metres up in the forest east of Missoula. It was founded in the 1890s around a gold mine and at its peak, around 1898, had about a thousand residents, with hotels, stores, saloons and a school. As the gold ran out, the town emptied, and a fire in 1912 destroyed many of its buildings; a short revival in the 1930s brought miners back for a few years. Today around thirty buildings survive, including the three-storey Wells Hotel, cabins and a general store, looked after by the Bureau of Land Management together with a preservation group. Because it lies at the end of a mountain road, far from any highway, Garnet has a quiet and authentic atmosphere. In winter the road is closed to cars, and visitors arrive on snowmobiles or skis; two cabins can be rented for overnight stays.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Missoula Montana Airport (MSO), about 65 km, allow about 1¼ hours. The last 18 km is a gravel mountain road. In winter the road is closed to cars, and the town is reached by snowmobile or on skis.

## Visiting

Garnet is open all year for a small fee, and a visitor centre is staffed in summer. There are no services, so bring water and food. Please treat the old buildings with care.
