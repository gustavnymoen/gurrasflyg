# Eastern State Penitentiary

## Overview

Eastern State Penitentiary in Philadelphia opened in 1829 and became one of the most famous and influential prisons in the world. Its design, with long cellblocks radiating from a central hub like the spokes of a wheel, was copied by hundreds of prisons. It was built for the "Pennsylvania system" of solitary confinement, in which prisoners lived alone in their cells, meant to reflect on their crimes and become penitent. Over time the system was abandoned, and the prison became overcrowded. Its famous inmates included the gangster Al Capone, whose surprisingly comfortable cell has been recreated. The prison closed in 1971 and stood empty for two decades, its cellblocks crumbling and filling with trees. Since 1994 it has been open as a museum in a state of preserved ruin, with exhibitions about the history of prisons and justice in the United States.

## Getting there

- **Train:** from Philadelphia International Airport (PHL), about 15 km away, the airport rail line reaches Center City in about 25 minutes; from there, take a bus or walk about 25 minutes to the Fairmount neighbourhood.
- **Taxi:** about 25 minutes from the airport.

## Visiting

The museum is open daily with timed tickets, and an audio tour is included. Allow about two hours. A large Halloween event runs in the autumn. Exhibitions also look at mass incarceration in the United States today.
