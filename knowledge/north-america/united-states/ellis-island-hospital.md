# Ellis Island Hospital

## Overview

Ellis Island in New York Harbor was the main immigration station of the United States from 1892 to 1954, and around 12 million people passed through it. While the main building on the north side of the island is now the famous National Museum of Immigration, the south side holds a far less known complex: the Ellis Island Immigrant Hospital. Opened in 1902 and expanded over the following decades, it had general wards, contagious-disease wards, operating rooms and a morgue, and it treated immigrants who arrived sick, many of them children. It was one of the largest public health hospitals in the country. After the immigration station closed, the 29 hospital buildings were abandoned and slowly decayed. Since 2014 a non-profit organisation has run guided hard-hat tours through the corridors, where peeling walls, empty wards and large photographic artworks of immigrants bring the past back to life.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR) is about 15 km away; John F. Kennedy International Airport is another option.
- **Train and ferry:** take a train into Manhattan or to Jersey City, then the official ferry from Battery Park or from Liberty State Park, which stops at Liberty Island and Ellis Island.

## Visiting

The hospital can only be visited on hard-hat tours of about 90 minutes in small groups; book well ahead, as the tour ticket includes the ferry. There is airport-style security screening before boarding the ferry. Wear comfortable shoes; some areas are dusty and uneven.
