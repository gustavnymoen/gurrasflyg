# Alcatraz

## Overview

Alcatraz Island lies in the middle of San Francisco Bay, about 2 km from the city's waterfront. It served as a military fort and a military prison before becoming a maximum-security federal penitentiary in 1934, intended for prisoners who had caused trouble elsewhere. Its notorious inmates included Al Capone, George "Machine Gun" Kelly and Robert Stroud, the "Birdman of Alcatraz". The cold water and strong currents made escape almost impossible; in the most famous attempt, in 1962, three men disappeared and were never found. The prison closed in 1963 because it had become too expensive to run. From 1969 to 1971 the island was occupied by Native American activists, whose painted messages can still be seen. Today it is part of a national recreation area visited by more than a million people a year. In 2025 the US government announced plans to reopen Alcatraz as a prison, so its future as a visitor site is uncertain.

## Getting there

- **Train and walk:** from San Francisco International Airport (SFO), about 25 km away, take BART to Embarcadero, about 35 minutes, then walk about 15 minutes to Pier 33.
- **Ferry:** the official ferry from Pier 33 takes about 15 minutes to the island.

## Visiting

Tickets often sell out weeks in advance in summer; book through the official ferry operator. Allow about three hours including the ferry. The cellhouse audio tour is included. Wear layers, as it is often cold and windy. Check the current status before planning a visit, given the plans to reopen the prison.
