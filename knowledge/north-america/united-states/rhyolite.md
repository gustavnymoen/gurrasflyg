# Rhyolite

## Overview

Rhyolite is a ghost town in the Nevada desert, just outside Death Valley National Park. After gold was discovered in the surrounding hills in 1904, a town of several thousand people sprang up almost overnight, with banks, a stock exchange, an opera house, three railway lines and concrete buildings that were meant to last. The boom was short: the mines produced less than hoped, the financial panic of 1907 dried up investment, and by 1920 the town was almost empty. Today the ruins include the shell of the three-storey Cook Bank building, the old school, the railway depot and a house built from tens of thousands of glass bottles. Next to the town is the Goldwell Open Air Museum, where ghostly sculptures, including a version of the Last Supper, stand in the desert. Rhyolite is often visited on the way into Death Valley.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Harry Reid International Airport (LAS) in Las Vegas, about 190 km, allow two hours via US Highway 95 to Beatty. Rhyolite lies about 6 km west of Beatty.

## Visiting

The ruins are freely accessible all year; some buildings are fenced off. In summer, temperatures can rise above 40 °C, so bring plenty of water. Watch out for rattlesnakes and old mine shafts, and stay on the tracks.
