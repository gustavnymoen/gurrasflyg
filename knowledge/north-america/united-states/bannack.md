# Bannack

## Overview

Bannack is a ghost town in south-western Montana, founded after gold was discovered on Grasshopper Creek in 1862. Within a year it had several thousand residents, and in 1864 it briefly became the first capital of the new Montana Territory. Its early years were violent: the town's sheriff, Henry Plummer, was accused of leading a gang of road agents and was hanged by vigilantes in 1864. Mining continued on a smaller scale for decades, but the population gradually dwindled, and the last residents left in the 1970s. Bannack is now a state park, with around sixty log, brick and frame buildings along the main street, including the old courthouse that became the Hotel Meade, a Masonic lodge and schoolhouse, saloons and the jail. Many of the buildings can be entered, giving a vivid picture of life in a frontier mining town.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** the nearest airport is Butte (BTM), about 120 km away. Bozeman (BZN), about 200 km away, has more flights.
- **Car:** from Butte, allow about 1½ hours via Dillon. The last part is a gravel road.

## Visiting

The park is open all year with an entry fee, and many buildings can be entered. In July, a living-history festival brings the town back to life. Services are limited, so bring food and water.
