# St. Elmo

## Overview

St. Elmo is a former gold- and silver-mining town in the Sawatch Range of the Rocky Mountains in Colorado, at about 3,000 metres above sea level. Founded in 1880, it grew to around two thousand people, with hotels, saloons, a dance hall and a newspaper, and it was a stop on a railway line that tunnelled under the Continental Divide. When the mines declined and the railway closed in the 1920s, people moved away, and St. Elmo became a ghost town. Unusually, many of its wooden buildings along the main street still stand, including the general store, the town hall and the schoolhouse. Some are privately owned, and the general store opens as a shop in summer. The town is also known for the chipmunks that dart along its boardwalks. Surrounded by forest and high peaks, St. Elmo is a popular starting point for off-road trails.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** the nearest airport is Gunnison-Crested Butte (GUC), about 110 km away. Denver International Airport (DEN), about 240 km away, has many more flights.
- **Car:** from Denver, allow about 3½ hours. The last 20 km is a gravel road, which can be snowy in winter.

## Visiting

The town is freely accessible, but most buildings are private and can only be seen from outside. The general store is open in summer. At this altitude, take it easy and be ready for sudden changes in the weather.
