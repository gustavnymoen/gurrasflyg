# Bannerman Castle

## Overview

Bannerman Castle stands on Pollepel Island, a small rocky island in the Hudson River about 90 km north of New York City. In 1900 Francis Bannerman VI, a dealer who had grown rich buying up military surplus after the Spanish-American War, bought the island to store his huge stock of weapons and ammunition. He designed a castle-like arsenal in the style of the Scottish castles of his ancestors, with towers, turrets and his family name in giant letters on the wall, as an advertisement to passing boats and trains. Bannerman died in 1918, and in 1920 an explosion of stored ammunition damaged the buildings; a fire in 1969 left them a roofless shell. The island is now owned by New York State, and a trust stabilises the ruins and runs tours, theatre performances and events in the restored gardens.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** the nearest airport is New York Stewart International (SWF), about 20 km away. John F. Kennedy International (JFK), about 110 km away, has many more flights.
- **Train:** Metro-North trains run from Grand Central Terminal to Beacon in about 1½ hours.
- **Boat tour:** guided tours leave by boat from Beacon; kayak tours are also offered.

## Visiting

The island can only be visited on guided tours and at events, from about May to October; book ahead. The ruins are fenced off, so follow the paths, which include steep steps.
