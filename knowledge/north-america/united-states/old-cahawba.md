# Old Cahawba

## Overview

Old Cahawba is the site of Alabama's first permanent state capital, where the Alabama and Cahaba rivers meet in the centre of the state. The town was founded in 1819 and served as capital in the early 1820s, until the legislature moved away after repeated floods. Cahawba later revived as a busy river port for shipping cotton, and during the American Civil War a Confederate prison there held thousands of Union soldiers. After the war, floods, disease and the loss of the county seat drove people away, and by the early twentieth century Cahawba was a ghost town. Today it is an archaeological park where visitors walk along the old street grid past brick columns standing alone in the fields, the ruins of mansions, old cemeteries and an artesian well. Few buildings remain, but the landscape is evocative, and the site is known for its ghost stories.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Montgomery Regional Airport (MGM), about 80 km, allow 1¼ hours via Selma. There is no public transport.

## Visiting

The park is open daily; check the opening hours of the welcome centre. In summer it is hot and humid, with mosquitoes, so bring water and repellent. Flooding occasionally closes the access roads.
