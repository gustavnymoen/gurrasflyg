# Plymouth

## Overview

Plymouth was the capital of Montserrat, a small British Overseas Territory in the Caribbean, until the Soufrière Hills volcano awoke in 1995 after centuries of quiet. As the eruptions continued, the town and the southern part of the island were evacuated, and in 1997 pyroclastic flows and mudflows buried Plymouth under metres of ash and rock. Only the upper floors of some buildings, church towers and the outlines of streets and houses still stand out from the grey deposits. About two-thirds of Montserrat's population left the island, and the south remains an exclusion zone where nobody may live. The volcano is monitored around the clock by the Montserrat Volcano Observatory. Visitors can see Plymouth from viewpoints in the north, such as Garibaldi Hill, or enter parts of the zone on authorised guided tours when the volcano is quiet. It is often called a modern Pompeii.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** John A. Osborne Airport (MNI) on Montserrat has short flights from Antigua's V. C. Bird International Airport (ANU), the main gateway.
- **Ferry:** on some days, a ferry runs from Antigua to Montserrat in about 1½ hours.
- **Car:** on the island, use a taxi or a local guide to reach the viewpoints.

## Visiting

The exclusion zone can only be entered on authorised tours, which depend on volcanic activity and can be cancelled at short notice. The viewpoints in the north are freely accessible. The observatory's visitor centre explains the eruption and the monitoring.
