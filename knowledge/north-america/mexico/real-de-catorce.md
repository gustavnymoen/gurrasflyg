# Real de Catorce

## Overview

Real de Catorce is an old silver-mining town high in the Sierra de Catorce in the state of San Luis Potosí, central Mexico, at about 2,700 metres above sea level. Founded in the late eighteenth century after rich silver deposits were found, it became one of the wealthiest mining towns of New Spain, with a large church, a mint and grand stone houses. When silver prices collapsed in the early twentieth century and the Mexican Revolution followed, the mines closed and the population fell from many thousands to a few hundred, leaving whole streets in ruins. The town is reached through the Ogarrio Tunnel, a 2.3 km single-lane former mining tunnel. Today Real de Catorce attracts pilgrims to its church of St Francis, tourists exploring the ruined mines and the ghost-town quarter, and the Wixárika people, for whom the desert below is sacred.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** San Luis Potosí International Airport (SLP) is about 250 km away.
- **Bus:** take a bus to Matehuala, about three hours, then a local bus through the tunnel to Real de Catorce, about 1½ hours.
- **Car:** about 3½ hours from the airport. The last part is a cobbled road and the single-lane tunnel, with traffic alternating in each direction.

## Visiting

The town is open to visitors and lives from tourism. Jeep and horseback tours go to the ruined mines and the desert. Nights are cold at this altitude. Respect the sacred sites of the Wixárika people; collecting plants in the desert is forbidden.
