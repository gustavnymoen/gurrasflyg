# Whalers Bay

## Overview

Whalers Bay lies inside Deception Island, in the South Shetland Islands off the Antarctic Peninsula. The island is the rim of an active volcano whose crater has been flooded by the sea, and ships enter it through a narrow gap called Neptune's Bellows. From 1912 to 1931 a Norwegian company ran a whaling station on the black volcanic beach, boiling whale blubber into oil in huge iron tanks. In 1928 the Australian explorer Hubert Wilkins made the first powered flight in Antarctica from the island. A British research base was later set up at the old station, but volcanic eruptions in 1967 and 1969 sent mud and ash through the buildings, and the base was abandoned. Today the rusting tanks and boilers, collapsed buildings, an aircraft hangar and a small cemetery lie along the steaming shore. Whalers Bay is a protected historic site under the Antarctic Treaty, and visitors must follow strict rules.

## Getting there

- **Plane and cruise:** there is no airport. Most visitors arrive on expedition cruises that start in Ushuaia, Argentina (USH); crossing the Drake Passage takes about two days.
- **Fly and cruise:** some cruises start with a flight from Punta Arenas in Chile to King George Island, then continue by ship.

## Visiting

Passengers go ashore by inflatable boat during the Antarctic summer, from November to March. Do not touch or enter the historic structures, and keep a safe distance from seals and penguins. The water near the shore is warmed by the volcano, and some cruises let passengers take a short swim.
