# Grytviken

## Overview

Grytviken is an abandoned whaling station on the island of South Georgia in the South Atlantic, more than 1,400 km east of the Falkland Islands. It was founded in 1904 by the Norwegian whaler Carl Anton Larsen as the first shore-based whaling station in Antarctic waters, and it processed tens of thousands of whales before closing in 1966. Rusting boilers, fuel tanks, slipways and the hulls of old whale catchers still line the shore of King Edward Cove, while elephant seals and fur seals have taken over the beaches. The polar explorer Sir Ernest Shackleton is buried in the small whalers' cemetery, and the restored Norwegian church still holds occasional services. The South Georgia Museum, in the former station manager's house, tells the story of whaling and exploration. Grytviken is one of the most remote abandoned industrial sites in the world, and it can only be reached by sea.

## Getting there

- **Plane and cruise:** there is no airport on South Georgia. Most visitors arrive on expedition cruises that start in Ushuaia, Argentina (USH), often combined with the Falkland Islands and the Antarctic Peninsula.
- **From the Falklands:** some cruises start in the Falkland Islands, which have flights to Mount Pleasant Airport (MPN). The crossing to South Georgia takes about two days.

## Visiting

Passengers go ashore by inflatable boat under strict biosecurity rules: boots and clothing are cleaned before landing to protect the island's wildlife. Keep a safe distance from fur seals, which can be aggressive in the breeding season. The whaling station's buildings can be seen from outside; the museum, church and cemetery are open during cruise visits. Weather can cancel landings at short notice.
