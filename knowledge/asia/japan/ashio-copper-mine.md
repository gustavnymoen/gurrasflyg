# Ashio Copper Mine

## Overview

The Ashio Copper Mine lies in a mountain valley in Nikkō, in Tochigi Prefecture north of Tokyo. Copper was mined here from the seventeenth century, and after the Furukawa company took over in 1877 it became the largest copper mine in Japan, supplying a country that was industrialising fast. The growth came at a terrible cost: smoke from the smelter killed the forests on the surrounding mountains, and waste in the Watarase River poisoned fields and fish downstream, causing what is considered Japan's first major industrial pollution disaster. The politician Tanaka Shōzō led a famous campaign for the victims. Mining ended in 1973. Today visitors can ride a small train into part of the old tunnels, where life-size figures show how miners worked through the centuries, and see the remains of the smelter and miners' housing in the town. Replanting has slowly brought the bare mountainsides back to green.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** Tokyo Haneda Airport (HND) is about 170 km away.
- **Train:** travel via Tokyo to Kiryū, about two hours, then take the Watarase Keikoku Railway up the valley to Tsūdō station, about 1½ hours.

## Visiting

The mine tour is open daily with an entry fee and takes about an hour; it is cool inside the tunnels. The smelter ruins and the old town can be explored on foot. A visit combines well with the temples of Nikkō.
