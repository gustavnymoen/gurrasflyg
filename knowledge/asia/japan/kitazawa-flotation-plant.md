# Kitazawa Flotation Plant

## Overview

The Kitazawa Flotation Plant stands in the town of Aikawa on Sado Island, in the Sea of Japan off Niigata. Sado was one of Japan's most important sources of gold and silver for nearly four centuries, from the early 1600s until the last mine closed in 1989. In the late 1930s, as Japan prepared for war, a modern flotation plant was built at Kitazawa to process ore on a huge scale, said at the time to be the largest of its kind in East Asia. Its stepped concrete terraces climb the hillside, and since the plant closed they have slowly been taken over by trees and ivy, which has led people to compare them to the floating castle in a Studio Ghibli film. The plant is part of the Sado Island Gold Mines UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 2024, together with the historic mine tunnels nearby; the listing also prompted debate about Korean labourers who worked in the mines during the war.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** Niigata Airport (KIJ) is the nearest airport; take a bus or taxi to Niigata port, about 25 minutes.
- **Ferry:** jetfoils reach Ryōtsu on Sado in about an hour, and car ferries in about 2½ hours.
- **Bus:** from Ryōtsu, buses reach Aikawa in about an hour.

## Visiting

The ruins can be seen freely from the paths around them, but the structures themselves cannot be entered. The historic mine tunnels nearby can be visited on tours with an entry fee. In the evening the ruins are lit up.
