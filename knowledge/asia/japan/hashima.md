# Hashima

## Overview

Hashima is a small island about 15 km from the port of Nagasaki in southern Japan. From the late nineteenth century, the Mitsubishi company mined coal from beneath the sea floor here and gradually enlarged the island with sea walls and reclaimed land. To house the miners and their families, it built some of Japan's first large reinforced-concrete apartment blocks, and at its peak around 1960 more than 5,000 people lived on an island only about 480 metres long, one of the highest population densities ever recorded. Its high walls and blocks give it the silhouette of a warship, hence its nickname Gunkanjima, "Battleship Island". When Japan switched from coal to oil, the mine closed in 1974 and the island was abandoned within weeks. Since 2015 it has been part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site, though the listing is controversial because Korean and Chinese forced labourers worked in the mine during the Second World War.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** Nagasaki Airport (NGS) is about 40 km away; Fukuoka (FUK) has more international flights.
- **Bus:** the airport bus reaches Nagasaki city in about 45 minutes.
- **Boat tour:** licensed tour boats leave from Nagasaki port and take about 40–50 minutes each way.

## Visiting

The island can only be visited on licensed boat tours; book ahead. Visitors may only walk along a short route of walkways at the southern end. Landings are often cancelled when the sea is rough, and the boat then circles the island instead.
