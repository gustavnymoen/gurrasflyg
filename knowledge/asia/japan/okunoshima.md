# Okunoshima

## Overview

Okunoshima is a small island in the Seto Inland Sea, off the coast of Hiroshima Prefecture in western Japan. From 1929 until 1945 it was the site of a secret factory where the Japanese army produced poison gas, including mustard gas, much of which was used in China. The island was removed from maps, and the workers, many of whom later suffered from long-term illnesses, were sworn to secrecy. After the war the factory was dismantled and the gas stocks were destroyed or dumped. Ruins of the power station, storage buildings and gun emplacements still stand among the trees, and a small Poison Gas Museum tells the island's dark history and the story of its victims. Today Okunoshima is better known as "Rabbit Island", because of the hundreds of wild rabbits that roam freely and approach visitors, a strange contrast to the ruins.

## Getting there

- **Bus and train:** from Hiroshima Airport (HIJ), take a bus or taxi to Mihara station, about 35 minutes, then a local train to Tadanoumi, about 25 minutes.
- **Ferry:** ferries from Tadanoumi reach Okunoshima in about 15 minutes.

## Visiting

The island is open to visitors, and there is a hotel. The museum charges a small entry fee. Follow the local rules on feeding the rabbits. Some ruins are unstable; do not enter fenced-off buildings.
