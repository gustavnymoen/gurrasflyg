# Shuinandong Smelter

## Overview

The Shuinandong Smelter, popularly known as the "Thirteen Levels", is a huge ruined industrial building terraced into the mountainside above the sea at Ruifang, on Taiwan's north-east coast. It was built by a Japanese mining company in the 1930s, when Taiwan was under Japanese rule, to refine ore from the gold and copper mines of nearby Jinguashi. After the war it was taken over by a Taiwanese state mining company and closed in the 1980s. Its stepped grey walls look like a ruined fortress from a fantasy film, and long abandoned exhaust pipelines climb the mountain behind it. Below, the water of the so-called Yin-Yang Sea is coloured yellow by minerals washing down from the hills. The building itself is closed, but it can be seen from the coastal road and nearby viewpoints, and on some evenings it is lit up with coloured lights.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** Taipei Songshan Airport (TSA) is about 35 km away; Taiwan Taoyuan International (TPE) is the main international airport.
- **Train and bus:** take a train from Taipei to Ruifang, about 45 minutes, then a bus towards Jinguashi and Shuinandong, about 30 minutes.

## Visiting

The smelter can only be seen from outside, from the road and viewpoints. A visit combines well with the Gold Museum in Jinguashi and the old mountain town of Jiufen.
