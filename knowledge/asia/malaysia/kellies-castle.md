# Kellie's Castle

## Overview

Kellie's Castle stands on a small hill near Batu Gajah in the state of Perak, Malaysia, surrounded by former rubber plantations. It was begun around 1915 by William Kellie Smith, a Scottish planter who had made his fortune in rubber and tin, as a grand home for his family, combining Scottish, Moorish and Indian architectural styles. He brought workers from India to build it, and many of them died during an outbreak of disease; in their memory, he built a Hindu temple nearby. In 1926 Kellie Smith died of pneumonia on a trip to Portugal, and his family returned to Scotland, leaving the mansion unfinished. Since then its red-brick walls, arched windows, rooftop terrace and the shaft of what was planned as one of the first lifts in Malaya have stood empty. Rumours of secret tunnels and ghosts have added to its fame, and it has appeared in films.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** Sultan Azlan Shah Airport (IPH) in Ipoh is about 20 km away. Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KUL), about 200 km away, has many more flights.
- **Car or taxi:** about 25 minutes from Ipoh. Trains run from Kuala Lumpur to Ipoh in about 2½ hours.

## Visiting

The castle is open daily with an entry fee; allow about an hour. Stairs lead up to the roof terrace. It is hot, so bring water.
