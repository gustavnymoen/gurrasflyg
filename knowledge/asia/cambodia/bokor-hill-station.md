# Bokor Hill Station

## Overview

Bokor Hill Station sits on the plateau of Bokor Mountain, about 1,000 metres above sea level in Preah Monivong National Park, near Kampot in southern Cambodia. It was built by the French colonial authorities in the 1920s as a cool retreat from the heat of the lowlands, with a grand hotel and casino, a Catholic church, a post office and villas; hundreds of workers are said to have died building it. The resort was abandoned in the 1940s, briefly revived and abandoned again, and in the 1970s the Khmer Rouge occupied the plateau, followed by years of fighting with Vietnamese forces. For decades the misty, moss-covered ruins were one of Cambodia's eeriest sights. Since the 2010s a large development has brought a new road, a modern casino resort and new buildings to the mountain, and the old Bokor Palace hotel has been renovated, but the abandoned church and other ruins still stand.

## Getting there

- **Car or taxi:** from Sihanouk International Airport (KOS), about 110 km, allow about two hours to Kampot, then about an hour up the mountain road.

## Visiting

The plateau is open to visitors, and the old church and ruins can be explored freely. Fog and rain are common, and it is much cooler than in the lowlands. Bring a jacket.
