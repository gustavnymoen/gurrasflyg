# Corregidor

## Overview

Corregidor is a small, tadpole-shaped island at the entrance to Manila Bay in the Philippines, about 50 km west of Manila. Because it controls access to the bay, it was fortified for centuries, and under American rule in the early twentieth century it became Fort Mills, with huge gun batteries, barracks and a network of tunnels. In 1942, after Japan invaded the Philippines, American and Filipino troops held out on Corregidor for months under constant bombardment before surrendering in May; in 1945 American forces retook the island in a fierce battle. Today the island is a memorial and historic site. Visitors tour the shell-scarred ruins of the Mile-Long Barracks, rusting gun emplacements, the Pacific War Memorial and the Malinta Tunnel, which served as headquarters and hospital during the siege and now hosts a light-and-sound show.

## Getting there

- **Taxi:** from Ninoy Aquino International Airport (MNL), take a taxi to the ferry terminal in Manila, about 30 minutes.
- **Ferry:** day-tour ferries from Manila reach Corregidor in about 1¼ hours.

## Visiting

Most visitors come on organised day tours that include the ferry, a guided tram tour of the island and lunch; overnight stays are possible at the island's hotel. It is hot and humid, so bring sun protection and water. Corregidor is a war memorial, so visit respectfully.
