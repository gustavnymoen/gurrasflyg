# Houtouwan

## Overview

Houtouwan is a former fishing village on Shengshan Island, part of the Shengsi archipelago at the mouth of the Yangtze River, east of Shanghai. In its heyday the village had more than two thousand residents, who lived in stone houses built on steep slopes above a small bay. From the 1990s, as fishing declined and the island's remote location made schooling and supplies difficult, families moved to the mainland, and by the early 2000s only a handful of people remained. Over the following years, climbing plants spread over the empty houses until whole streets were covered in green, turning Houtouwan into one of the most photographed abandoned places in China. Since the mid-2010s it has been run as a tourist site with an entry fee, walkways and viewing platforms, and some of the houses in the area have been turned into guesthouses and cafés.

## Getting there

- **Bus or taxi:** from Shanghai Pudong International Airport (PVG), travel to one of the ferry terminals south of Shanghai, about an hour.
- **Ferry:** take a ferry to the Shengsi islands, about two hours, then a local ferry or bus connection to Shengshan Island.

## Visiting

The village is open daily with an entry fee, and visitors follow the walkways; do not enter the houses. Ferries depend on the weather and can be cancelled, and they are busy during holidays, so book ahead and plan to stay overnight.
