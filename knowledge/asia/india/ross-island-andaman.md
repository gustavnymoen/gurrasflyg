# Ross Island

## Overview

Ross Island, officially renamed Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose Dweep in 2018, is a small island near Port Blair in India's Andaman and Nicobar Islands. From 1858 it was the administrative headquarters of the British penal settlement in the Andamans, where prisoners from the Indian independence movement were held. On this tiny island the British built a self-contained colonial town for officials and their families, with a church, a bakery, a ballroom, a club, a hospital and the Chief Commissioner's residence, earning it the nickname "Paris of the East". An earthquake in 1941 damaged the buildings, and during the Second World War Japanese forces occupied the Andamans and built bunkers on the island. After the war it was abandoned. Today the ruins are wrapped in the roots of huge banyan and peepal trees, and spotted deer and peacocks wander among them.

## Getting there

- **Taxi:** from Veer Savarkar International Airport (IXZ) in Port Blair, take a taxi to the jetty, about 15 minutes.
- **Ferry:** boats cross to the island in about 15 minutes.

## Visiting

The island is open during the day with an entry fee, and a light-and-sound show tells its history in the evening. It is hot and humid, so bring water. Please do not feed the deer.
