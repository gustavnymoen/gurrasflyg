# Dhanushkodi

## Overview

Dhanushkodi lies at the end of a narrow spit of sand on Rameswaram Island, at the south-eastern tip of India in Tamil Nadu, only about 30 km from Sri Lanka. In the early twentieth century it was a busy town with a railway station, a port, a church, a post office and a ferry service to Sri Lanka. On the night of 22 December 1964 a powerful cyclone struck with huge waves, destroying the town and killing many people, including the passengers of a train that was swept into the sea. The government declared the town unfit to live in, and it was never rebuilt. Today the ruins of the church, the railway station and other buildings stand among sand dunes between the Bay of Bengal and the Indian Ocean. In 2017 a new road opened to the tip of the spit, and the area is also an important Hindu pilgrimage place linked to the Ramayana.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** Madurai Airport (IXM) is about 190 km away.
- **Train or bus:** travel to Rameswaram, about four hours.
- **Bus, jeep or taxi:** continue about 20 km along the new road to Dhanushkodi.

## Visiting

The area can be visited during the day; there are no services at night. The sea has strong currents, and swimming is dangerous. Bring water and sun protection, as there is little shade.
