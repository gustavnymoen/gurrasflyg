# Villa Epecuén

## Overview

Villa Epecuén was a spa town on the shore of Lake Epecuén, near the town of Carhué in the west of Buenos Aires Province, about 550 km from the city of Buenos Aires. The lake's water is extremely salty, and from the 1920s visitors came from all over Argentina to bathe in it, believing it could heal skin and joint problems. The small town had hotels, guesthouses, restaurants and shops, and in summer thousands of tourists filled its streets. After years of unusually heavy rain, the lake kept rising, and in November 1985 the embankment protecting the town broke. Water flooded the streets, and within years the town lay several metres under the lake. From around 2009 the water slowly drew back, revealing streets of ruined buildings and dead trees, all bleached white by salt. Pablo Novak, who lived alone near the ruins for decades until his death in 2022, became famous as the town's last resident.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Comandante Espora Airport (BHI) in Bahía Blanca, about 250 km, allow three hours; from Buenos Aires-Ezeiza (EZE) the drive takes about seven hours.
- **Bus:** long-distance buses run from Buenos Aires to Carhué; from Carhué the ruins are about 10 km away by taxi or bicycle.

## Visiting

The ruins are open and free to explore on foot. There is little shade and the salt reflects the sun strongly, so bring sun protection and water. A small museum in Carhué tells the town's story. Do not climb on the unstable walls.
