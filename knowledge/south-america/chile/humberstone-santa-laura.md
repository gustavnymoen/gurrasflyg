# Humberstone and Santa Laura

## Overview

Humberstone and Santa Laura are two abandoned saltpetre works in the Atacama Desert of northern Chile, about 45 km inland from the port of Iquique. From the late nineteenth century, saltpetre (sodium nitrate) dug from the desert was in huge demand around the world as fertiliser and for explosives, and hundreds of works like these brought great wealth to Chile. Thousands of workers from Chile, Peru and Bolivia lived in company towns in the middle of the desert, with their own culture and traditions. When synthetic nitrate was invented in Germany, the industry collapsed, and both works closed by 1960. Humberstone, named after the British engineer James Humberstone, still has its theatre, church, market, hotel and a swimming pool built from the iron hull of a ship. At Santa Laura, about 1.5 km away, the tall processing plant and its chimney still stand. Together they became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2005.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Diego Aracena International Airport (IQQ), south of Iquique, allow about an hour.
- **Bus:** buses from Iquique towards Pozo Almonte pass the site in about 45 minutes; ask the driver to stop at Humberstone.

## Visiting

The site is open daily with an entry fee, which also covers Santa Laura. The desert sun is fierce and there is little shade, so bring water, a hat and sun protection, and plan two to three hours. Do not climb on the old machinery.
