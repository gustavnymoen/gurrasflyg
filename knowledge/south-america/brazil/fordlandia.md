# Fordlândia

## Overview

Fordlândia is a town on the banks of the Tapajós River in the Brazilian state of Pará, deep in the Amazon rainforest. In 1928 the American car maker Henry Ford bought a huge area of land here to grow rubber for car tyres, and his company built a complete American town in the jungle, with wooden bungalows, a hospital, a school, a sawmill, a water tower and even a golf course. The project went wrong from the start. The rubber trees, planted close together, were destroyed by leaf blight and insects, and Brazilian workers resented the strict American rules on food, alcohol and daily life, which led to a riot in 1930. Ford moved the plantation downriver to Belterra in the 1930s and sold the land back to Brazil in 1945 at a large loss. Today a few thousand people live among the ruins, and the rusting factory, water tower and empty American houses still stand.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** Santarém Airport (STM) is the nearest airport, about 300 km downriver, with flights from Belém and Manaus.
- **Boat tour:** tour operators in Santarém arrange trips up the Tapajós; regular riverboats take most of a day.

## Visiting

Fordlândia is best visited on an organised tour from Santarém, which covers the boat trip and a local guide. Some old buildings are unsafe, so do not enter them without a guide. Bring insect repellent, sun protection and water.
