# Paranapiacaba

## Overview

Paranapiacaba is a small railway village on the edge of the Serra do Mar mountains, about 50 km south-east of São Paulo city. It was built in the second half of the nineteenth century by the British-owned São Paulo Railway, which ran the line carrying coffee from the São Paulo plateau down the steep escarpment to the port of Santos. Because the slope was too steep for ordinary trains, wagons were hauled up and down by cables and stationary steam engines, and the village housed the engineers and workers who ran them. Its wooden houses, straight streets and the station clock tower, often compared to Big Ben, give it a strangely English look, and it is often wrapped in thick fog. The cable system stopped working in the 1980s and the village declined, but it is now a protected heritage site. About a thousand people still live there, and a railway museum shows the old machinery.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** São Paulo-Congonhas Airport (CGH) is about 50 km away; most international flights land at São Paulo-Guarulhos (GRU).
- **Train:** take the city train line to Rio Grande da Serra, about an hour from central São Paulo.
- **Bus:** from Rio Grande da Serra, a local bus reaches Paranapiacaba in about 20 minutes.

## Visiting

The village can be explored freely, and the railway museum charges a small entry fee. Weekends are busiest, with cafés and guided walks. Bring a rain jacket, as fog and drizzle are common. Trails into the rainforest around the village require a local guide.
