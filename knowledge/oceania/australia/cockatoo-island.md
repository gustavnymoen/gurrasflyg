# Cockatoo Island

## Overview

Cockatoo Island is the largest island in Sydney Harbour, where the Parramatta and Lane Cove rivers meet, a short ferry ride from the city centre. From 1839 it was a prison for convicts, who were made to cut the sandstone, build the prison buildings and carve huge grain silos into the rock. Later it held a reformatory and an industrial school for girls. In 1857 the Fitzroy Dock was completed, and the island gradually became one of Australia's largest shipyards, building and repairing ships for the navy through both world wars. The shipyard closed in 1991, leaving behind slipways, workshops, cranes, tunnels and machinery. In 2010 the island became part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site that brings together Australia's most important convict sites. Today it is managed as a public place, with a campground and holiday houses, and its industrial halls are often used for art exhibitions and events.

## Getting there

- **Train:** from Sydney Kingsford Smith Airport (SYD), about 15 km, the airport train reaches Circular Quay in about 20 minutes.
- **Ferry:** ferries from Circular Quay reach Cockatoo Island in about 20 minutes.

## Visiting

Entry to the island is free. Guided tours and audio guides explain the convict and shipyard history. The island has cafés, a campground and holiday houses for overnight stays. Wear comfortable shoes; the paths and tunnels are uneven.
