# Denniston

## Overview

Denniston is an old coal-mining settlement on a high, windswept plateau about 600 metres above the sea, near Westport on the West Coast of New Zealand's South Island. High-quality coal was found here, and mining began in 1879. To bring the coal down from the plateau, engineers built the Denniston Incline, a railway so steep that loaded wagons were lowered by cables, using gravity, while the weight of the descending wagons pulled the empty ones back up. It dropped more than 500 metres and was one of the steepest railways of its kind in the world. For years it was also the only way up for supplies, and even for the miners' families. In the early 1900s about 1,500 people lived on the plateau in harsh, foggy weather. The incline closed in 1967, and the town has almost vanished. The story inspired Jenny Pattrick's novel The Denniston Rose.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Westport Airport (WSZ), about 25 km, allow 30 minutes; the road climbs up to the plateau from Waimangaroa.
- **Plane:** Westport has flights from Wellington; from Christchurch International Airport (CHC) the drive takes about four hours.

## Visiting

The plateau is open and free to visit. Walking tracks with information panels lead to the top of the incline, the old mine entrances and the town site. The weather changes fast, so bring warm and waterproof clothing. Keep away from old mine shafts.
