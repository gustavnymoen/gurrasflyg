# Slievemore Deserted Village

## Overview

The Deserted Village at Slievemore lies on the southern slope of Slievemore mountain on Achill Island, off the west coast of Ireland in County Mayo. Between 80 and 100 stone cottages stand in a long line beside an old track, their roofs long gone, with traces of the small fields their inhabitants once farmed. The village was largely abandoned in the nineteenth century, around the time of the Great Famine, when many families moved to the coast, emigrated or were evicted. Afterwards some families used the houses seasonally as "booley" homes when they brought their cattle up to summer pastures, a practice that continued into the twentieth century. Today the ruins are protected, and archaeologists study them at a field school each summer. With the Atlantic on one side and the bare mountain on the other, it is a hauntingly beautiful place.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Ireland West Airport Knock (NOC), about 110 km, allow 1½–2 hours. Achill Island is connected to the mainland by a bridge.
- **Bus:** buses run from Westport to Achill; from the bus stop you will need a taxi or a long walk to the village.

## Visiting

The village is freely accessible all year. Park near the cemetery at the eastern end and walk along the track. The ground is uneven and can be boggy, so wear walking boots. Please do not climb on the walls of the ruins.
