# Doel

## Overview

Doel is a small polder village on the left bank of the river Scheldt, next to the port of Antwerp and in the shadow of a nuclear power station. From the 1960s onwards, plans to expand the port meant that the village would have to make way for new docks, and over the following decades most of its residents were bought out and moved away. Hundreds of houses stood empty, and street artists from all over Europe covered their walls with murals, turning Doel into an open-air gallery and a well-known destination for urban explorers. A small group of residents refused to leave and fought the plans in court for years. More recently the authorities changed course: the village will no longer be demolished, and former residents can buy their old homes back. Doel now lives in an unusual state between ghost town and revival, watched over by its seventeenth-century windmill on the dyke.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** the nearest airport is Antwerp (ANR), about 25 km away, with few routes. Most international travellers fly to Brussels Airport (BRU), about 60 km away.
- **Car:** about 30 minutes from Antwerp or an hour from Brussels Airport.
- **Bus:** a local bus runs to Doel a few times a day; check the timetable before you travel.

## Visiting

The streets of the village are public and free to walk. Some houses are inhabited again, so respect residents' privacy and do not enter any buildings. The murals change over time as new artists paint over old works. A walk along the Scheldt dyke gives views of the river and the port.
