# Karosta Prison

## Overview

Karosta is a district of the Latvian port city of Liepāja that was built at the end of the nineteenth century as a naval base for the Russian Tsar's fleet. In the Soviet era it became a closed military town, home to thousands of sailors and their families, and Latvians needed special permission to enter. Its military prison, built around 1900, held disobedient soldiers and sailors under the Tsarist, Latvian, Nazi and Soviet regimes until the 1990s. Today the prison is a museum where guides tell its grim history, and some programmes let visitors experience the harsh treatment of Soviet-era prisoners or even spend a night in a cell. Around it, Karosta still has an atmosphere of decay, with empty apartment blocks, the golden domes of the St Nicholas Naval Cathedral and the broken concrete of the Northern Forts on the beach.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** Liepāja International Airport (LPX) is about 10 km away but has few flights. Most travellers fly to Riga (RIX), about 220 km away.
- **Bus:** buses run from Riga to Liepāja in about 3½ hours; a local bus or taxi continues to Karosta.

## Visiting

Guided tours of the prison run all year, with shorter hours in winter. Some interactive programmes are intense and not suitable for children. The Northern Forts on the beach are free to visit, but the ruins are unstable, especially near the water.
