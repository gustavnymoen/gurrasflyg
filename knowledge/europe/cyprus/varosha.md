# Varosha

## Overview

Varosha, known in Turkish as Maraş, is the southern quarter of the city of Famagusta on the east coast of Cyprus. In the early 1970s it was one of the most popular beach resorts in the Mediterranean, with high-rise hotels, restaurants and shops along a long sandy beach. During the 1974 conflict, when Turkish forces took control of the north of the island, its Greek Cypriot residents fled, and the district was fenced off. For more than 45 years nobody was allowed in, and Varosha became a famous ghost town of crumbling hotels, empty streets and abandoned shops. In 2020 the Turkish Cypriot authorities opened some streets and the beach to visitors, a step criticised by the United Nations and the Republic of Cyprus, and hundreds of thousands of people have since walked or cycled through. Most buildings remain closed and unsafe, and Varosha's future is still part of the unresolved Cyprus dispute.

## Getting there

- **Car or taxi:** from Larnaca International Airport (LCA), about 55 km, allow about an hour to Famagusta. You cross the line between the two parts of the island at a crossing point, so bring your passport.

## Visiting

The opened area can be visited during the day, and bicycles and electric carts can be hired. Only the designated streets are open; the buildings are closed and dangerous. Photography of military areas is forbidden. Check your government's travel advice about crossing between the two parts of Cyprus.
