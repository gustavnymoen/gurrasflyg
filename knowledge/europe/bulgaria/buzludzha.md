# Buzludzha Monument

## Overview

The Buzludzha Monument stands on a peak of the Balkan Mountains in central Bulgaria, about 1,400 metres above sea level. It was built by the Bulgarian Communist Party and opened in 1981 to commemorate the founding of a socialist party at a secret meeting on this spot in 1891. Shaped like a giant concrete saucer with a tall tower beside it, it housed a circular ceremonial hall decorated with huge mosaics of workers, soldiers and communist leaders, while red glass stars on the tower could be seen from far away. After the fall of communism in 1989 the state abandoned the building, which was looted, damaged by the weather and covered in graffiti, and it became one of the world's most famous abandoned places. Since the late 2010s a foundation has been working to protect the mosaics and save the structure, and the building has been sealed and guarded.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** the nearest airport is Plovdiv (PDV), about 110 km away, with few flights. Most international travellers fly to Sofia (SOF), about 200 km away.
- **Car:** about 1½ hours from Plovdiv or three hours from Sofia. The last part is a mountain road that may be closed by snow in winter.
- **Day tours:** several operators run day trips from Sofia and Plovdiv.

## Visiting

It is not possible to go inside: the building is sealed and guarded around the clock. Visitors can walk around the outside, and the views over the mountains are spectacular. The weather on the peak changes quickly, so bring warm clothing even in summer.
