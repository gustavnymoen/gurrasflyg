# Linnahall

## Overview

Linnahall is a huge concrete and limestone building on the shore of Tallinn Bay, just outside the old town of Estonia's capital. It was built as the V. I. Lenin Palace of Culture and Sport for the sailing events of the 1980 Moscow Olympics, which were held in Tallinn. Designed by the architects Raine Karp and Riina Altmäe, it is a low, stepped structure that rises from the waterfront like an ancient monument, with a concert hall for more than 4,000 people, an ice rink and a heliport. Concerts and events were held there until 2010, when it closed because of its poor condition. Since then the building has slowly decayed, with grass and graffiti on its broad terraces. Its roof and stairways remain open as a public promenade with views over the sea and the old town, while plans for its restoration are still being discussed.

## Getting there

- **Tram or taxi:** from Tallinn Airport (TLL), about 5 km away, take the tram or a taxi to the city centre in about 15–20 minutes, then walk a few minutes from the old town to the harbour.

## Visiting

The building itself is closed, but the roof terraces and stairways are open as a public space at all times. The surfaces are uneven, slippery in winter and without railings in places. It is a popular spot at sunset.
