# Imber

## Overview

Imber is a village in the middle of Salisbury Plain in Wiltshire, in southern England. In December 1943 its inhabitants were told to leave within weeks so that American troops could practise for the invasion of Europe, and they were never allowed to return. The British Army has used the village for training ever since. Most of the original houses have gone, replaced by empty concrete buildings used to rehearse street fighting, but the medieval church of St Giles, the old manor house and a few other buildings still stand. Former residents campaigned for decades to go home, and their story has made Imber a symbol of the villages lost to war. The army opens the roads to Imber on a few days each year, usually around Easter, in August and around Christmas, when services are held in the church and visitors come to see the empty village.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Bristol Airport (BRS), about 60 km, allow 1¼ hours. The roads across the training area are only open on the announced open days. From London Heathrow (LHR) allow about two hours.
- **Vintage bus:** on one day each summer, a volunteer-run vintage bus service, the Imberbus, runs to Imber from Warminster.

## Visiting

Imber can only be visited on the open days announced by the army; at all other times the area is a live firing range and entry is forbidden. Stay on the roads and marked areas, and never pick up objects from the ground. The church is open on these days.
