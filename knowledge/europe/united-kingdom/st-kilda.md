# St Kilda

## Overview

St Kilda is a group of islands in the Atlantic, about 65 km west of the Outer Hebrides, and the most remote part of the British Isles. People lived on the main island, Hirta, for thousands of years, surviving on seabirds, sheep and fishing. In the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, contact with the outside world, disease and emigration weakened the small community, and in 1930 the last 36 inhabitants asked to be evacuated to the mainland. Their village still stands: a curving street of stone cottages, a church and school, and more than a thousand small stone storage huts called cleits scattered across the hillsides. St Kilda is owned by the National Trust for Scotland and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site for both its nature and its culture, with huge colonies of gannets, puffins and fulmars on the highest sea cliffs in Britain.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** fly to Stornoway (SYY) on the Isle of Lewis, from Glasgow (GLA) or Edinburgh.
- **Car:** drive about 1½–2 hours south to Leverburgh on the Isle of Harris.
- **Boat tour:** day trips from Leverburgh take about 2½–3 hours each way, weather permitting, from about April to September.

## Visiting

Day trips give about four to five hours ashore on Hirta. Landings are often cancelled because of the weather, so plan spare days. A National Trust ranger meets visitors, and a small museum is housed in one of the cottages. Keep away from cliff edges and nesting seabirds.
