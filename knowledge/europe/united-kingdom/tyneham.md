# Tyneham

## Overview

Tyneham is a small village in a valley near the Dorset coast in southern England. In December 1943 its 225 residents left their homes so that the army could use the land for training, believing they would return after the war. They never did: the area became part of the Lulworth army ranges and is still used for gunnery practice. The village has slowly fallen into ruin, but the church and the schoolhouse have been restored and turned into small exhibitions about life in Tyneham, with the pupils' names still above the coat hooks. The roofless cottages, the old telephone box and the farm buildings give a quiet, melancholy picture of rural England frozen in time. A path leads from the village down to Worbarrow Bay, one of the most beautiful stretches of the Jurassic Coast.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Bournemouth Airport (BOH), about 45 km, allow an hour. There is no public transport to the village. From London Heathrow (LHR) allow about three hours.

## Visiting

The village and the range walks are open on most weekends and during school holidays, when there is no firing; check the Lulworth Ranges opening times before you go. Entry to the village is free. Stay on the marked paths: the ranges may contain unexploded shells.
