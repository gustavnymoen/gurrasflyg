# Maunsell Sea Forts

## Overview

The Maunsell Sea Forts are groups of steel towers standing on legs in the Thames Estuary, off the coasts of Kent and Essex. They were designed by the engineer Guy Maunsell and built in 1942 and 1943 to defend London against German bombers and the aircraft that dropped mines in the estuary. Each army fort consisted of seven towers linked by walkways, carrying guns, searchlights and living quarters for the soldiers. After the war the forts were abandoned, and in the 1960s some were taken over by pirate radio stations broadcasting pop music to Britain. Today the rusting towers at Red Sands and Shivering Sands look like giant creatures wading in the sea. A preservation group is working to restore Red Sands, and boat trips take visitors out to see the forts up close.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** the nearest airport is London Southend (SEN). Most international travellers arrive at London Heathrow (LHR).
- **Train:** trains from London reach the Kent coast and the Thames ports in about 1–1½ hours.
- **Boat tour:** trips to the forts run in summer from harbours on the Kent coast and the Thames; departure points vary by operator.

## Visiting

The forts are normally seen from the water only; the preservation group occasionally organises visits onto Red Sands. Trips depend on weather and tides and can be cancelled at short notice. Bring warm, windproof clothing, even in summer.
