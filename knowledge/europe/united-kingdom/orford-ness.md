# Orford Ness

## Overview

Orford Ness is a long shingle spit on the Suffolk coast in eastern England, separated from the mainland by the river Alde and Ore. For most of the twentieth century it was a secret military test site. Early radar experiments took place here in the 1930s, and from the 1950s the Atomic Weapons Research Establishment tested the components of nuclear bombs, without nuclear material, in concrete laboratories designed to contain accidental explosions. The most striking of them, with heavy concrete roofs raised on pillars, are known as "the pagodas". The military left in the 1980s, and in 1993 the National Trust bought the site. Today it is a nature reserve where rare shingle plants and birds live among crumbling bunkers, barracks and test cells, many of them deliberately left to decay. It is one of the strangest landscapes in Britain.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** the nearest airport is Norwich (NWI), about 80 km away. Most international travellers arrive at London Stansted (STN), about two hours away.
- **Train and taxi:** trains run via Ipswich to Wickham Market or Melton; from there, take a taxi to Orford Quay, about 20 minutes.
- **Ferry:** the National Trust ferry crosses from Orford Quay to the spit.

## Visiting

The site is open on selected days, mainly from spring to autumn, and the number of visitors per day is limited, so book the ferry crossing in advance. The marked routes run for several kilometres over shingle: wear sturdy shoes, bring water and stay on the paths.
