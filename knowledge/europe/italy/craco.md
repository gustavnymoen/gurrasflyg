# Craco

## Overview

Craco is a medieval town perched on a steep clay ridge in the Basilicata region of southern Italy, about 50 km from Matera. For centuries its stone houses, churches and palaces climbed the hill towards a Norman tower at the top. From the 1960s a series of landslides, made worse by building work and leaking water pipes, began to tear the ground apart beneath the town. After a flood in the early 1970s and an earthquake in 1980, the remaining residents were moved to a new settlement in the valley below. The old town has been empty ever since, its crumbling buildings silhouetted against the sky. Craco's dramatic setting has made it a popular film location, used for The Passion of the Christ and a James Bond film, among others. Today it is a protected site that can be visited on guided walks wearing a safety helmet.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Bari Karol Wojtyła Airport (BRI), about 120 km, allow 1¾ hours. There is no practical public transport to Craco.

## Visiting

Visits are only possible on guided tours, which follow a set route along the edge of the old town; book in advance. Tours last about an hour, and helmets are provided. Most of the town is unstable and closed, so stay with the guide and wear sturdy shoes.
