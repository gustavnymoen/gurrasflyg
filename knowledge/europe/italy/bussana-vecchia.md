# Bussana Vecchia

## Overview

Bussana Vecchia is a medieval hill village a few kilometres from Sanremo on the Italian Riviera. On the morning of 23 February 1887, a strong earthquake struck the Ligurian coast and badly damaged the village, killing many of the people gathered in the church for the Ash Wednesday mass. The survivors built a new Bussana closer to the sea and left the old village to decay, and for more than half a century it stood empty. In the late 1950s and the 1960s, artists from Italy and abroad began moving into the ruins, repairing houses without running water or electricity and creating an international artists' village. Their presence has never been fully legal, since the buildings belong to the state, but the community has stayed. Today visitors wander narrow lanes between roofless walls, studios, galleries and small cafés, with the shell of the old church at the top of the village.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** Nice Côte d'Azur Airport (NCE) is about 60 km away.
- **Train:** trains run from Nice to Sanremo in about an hour, usually with a change in Ventimiglia; from Sanremo, take a local bus or a taxi, about 15 minutes.
- **Car:** about an hour from the airport.

## Visiting

The village is freely accessible. Galleries and shops are open mainly in the tourist season. The lanes are steep, and the ruined church is fenced off. People live here, so respect their privacy and their homes.
