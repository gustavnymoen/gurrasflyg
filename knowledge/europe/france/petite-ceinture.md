# Petite Ceinture

## Overview

The Petite Ceinture, or "little belt", is a railway line that once circled Paris inside the old city walls. Opened in stages from 1852, it carried goods and passengers between the city's main stations and neighbourhoods, but the metro took away its passengers, and passenger trains stopped in 1934. Freight traffic slowly faded too, and by the 1990s most of the 32-kilometre ring had been abandoned. For years it was a hidden world of overgrown tracks, tunnels, graffiti and small gardens, explored by urban explorers and walkers even though it was off limits. The city of Paris has since opened several sections as public promenades and nature trails, where you can walk along the old tracks past former stations, some of them converted into cafés and venues. Other sections remain closed while the city decides on their future.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** Paris Orly (ORY) is the nearest airport, about 15 km away. Paris Charles de Gaulle (CDG) is the main international airport.
- **Metro:** the open sections are spread around the city and are easy to reach by metro; check the City of Paris website for the current list of open sections and entrances.

## Visiting

The open sections are free and usually open during the day, with opening hours similar to the city's parks. Closed sections and tunnels are off limits. Some former stations along the line are now bars and cultural venues that can be visited in the evening.
