# Oradour-sur-Glane

## Overview

Oradour-sur-Glane is a village in central France that has been left in ruins since 10 June 1944, when a unit of the Waffen-SS killed more than 640 of its inhabitants and set the village on fire. After the war, the French state decided that the ruins should be preserved as a memorial, and a new village was built beside them. Visitors walk along the original main street, past roofless houses, the remains of shops and workshops, rusting cars and sewing machines, and the tram tracks that once connected the village to Limoges. The church, where many of the women and children died, still stands as a burnt shell. An underground memorial centre, the Centre de la Mémoire, explains what happened and places it in the wider history of the war. Oradour is a place of remembrance rather than adventure, and it is visited quietly and respectfully by people from all over the world.

## Getting there

- **Car or taxi:** Oradour is about 20 km from Limoges-Bellegarde Airport (LIG), around 30 minutes by road.
- **Bus:** buses from Limoges run a few times a day; check the timetable before you travel.
- **Train:** if you arrive from elsewhere in France, trains run to Limoges, for example from Paris in about 3½ hours.

## Visiting

Visitors enter the ruins through the Centre de la Mémoire. Silence is requested in the martyred village: stay on the paths and do not touch or move objects in the ruins. The site is suitable for all ages, but the exhibition describes the massacre in detail. Allow at least two hours.
