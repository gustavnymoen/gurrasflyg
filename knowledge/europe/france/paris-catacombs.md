# Paris Catacombs

## Overview

The Catacombs of Paris are a small part of a vast network of tunnels and quarries beneath the city, where limestone was dug out for centuries to build Paris. At the end of the eighteenth century, when the city's overcrowded cemeteries had become a health hazard, the bones of several million Parisians were moved into the old quarries. Workers arranged skulls and thigh bones into walls and patterns and added plaques with poems and quotations about death. The official ossuary, about 1.5 km long and 20 metres below the streets, opened to visitors in the nineteenth century and is now run by the city as a museum. The much larger network of tunnels around it, explored by a secret community known as the "cataphiles", is closed to the public and illegal to enter.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** Paris Orly (ORY) is the nearest airport, about 15 km away. Paris Charles de Gaulle (CDG) is the main international airport.
- **Train:** from Orly, take the Orlyval shuttle and the RER B to Denfert-Rochereau, where the entrance is, in about 35 minutes. From Charles de Gaulle, the RER B runs directly to Denfert-Rochereau in about 45 minutes.

## Visiting

Timed tickets must be booked online, often well in advance. The visit takes about an hour and involves 131 steps down and 112 steps up. It is about 14 °C underground all year. The visit is not suitable for people with heart or breathing problems or claustrophobia. Only the official route is open; entering other tunnels is illegal.
