# Ouvrage Hackenberg (Maginot Line)

## Overview

The Ouvrage Hackenberg, near Veckring in Lorraine in north-eastern France, is the largest fortress of the Maginot Line, the chain of fortifications that France built along its border with Germany in the 1930s. Behind a ridge of hills, about 10 km of tunnels connect gun blocks, barracks, ammunition stores, a power station and kitchens, all buried deep underground. An electric railway carried ammunition and soldiers through the galleries, and around a thousand men could live inside. In 1940 the German army went around the Maginot Line instead of attacking it head-on, and in 1944 Hackenberg saw fighting during the American advance. Today a volunteer association maintains the fortress. Visitors ride the underground railway through the tunnels, see the machinery and gun turrets, and learn how life inside the fortress worked.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Luxembourg Airport (LUX), about 45 km, allow 45 minutes. There is no practical public transport to the fort.

## Visiting

The fortress can only be visited on guided tours, which run on set days, more often in summer. Allow about two hours. It is about 12 °C underground all year, so bring a jacket, and expect a fair amount of walking.
