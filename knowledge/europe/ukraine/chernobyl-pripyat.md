# Chernobyl Exclusion Zone and Pripyat

## Overview

The Chernobyl Exclusion Zone surrounds the nuclear power plant in northern Ukraine where reactor 4 exploded on 26 April 1986, the worst nuclear accident in history. The nearby city of Pripyat, home to about 49,000 people, was evacuated the following day and has never been resettled. Its apartment blocks, schools, hospital, swimming pool and the amusement park with its yellow Ferris wheel, which never opened, have become symbols of the disaster and of urban exploration itself. The zone also contains abandoned villages and the huge Duga radar, a Soviet early-warning antenna. Before 2022, licensed guides took large numbers of visitors on day tours from Kyiv, with radiation checks on the way out. Russian forces occupied the zone in early 2022, and since the war began tourist visits have been suspended. Chernobyl remains the most famous abandoned place in the world, but it cannot currently be visited.

## Getting there

- **Currently not possible:** tours are suspended, and civil flights to Ukraine are currently suspended too.
- **Before the war:** tours started in Kyiv, about 130 km to the south, with transport to the zone included. Most visitors flew into Kyiv Boryspil International Airport (KBP).

## Visiting

The zone is closed to visitors. Entering without a permit has always been illegal, and it is dangerous: parts of the zone are still contaminated, and since 2022 there is also a risk of unexploded ordnance. Follow official Ukrainian sources and your government's travel advice before making any plans.
