# Pyramiden

## Overview

Pyramiden is a former coal-mining settlement on Svalbard, named after the pyramid-shaped mountain above it. Founded by Sweden in 1910 and sold to the Soviet Union in 1927, it grew into a model Soviet town for around a thousand people, with a cultural palace, a sports hall, a heated swimming pool and what is often called the world's northernmost statue of Lenin. Mining stopped in 1998 and the town was emptied within months, leaving buildings, furniture and books behind. The dry, cold climate has preserved much of it, and Pyramiden is now one of the best-kept Soviet ghost towns anywhere. A small hotel has reopened, and a few caretakers live there in season and guide visitors through the empty streets. Surrounded by glaciers and mountains, and reached only by boat or snowmobile, a visit to Pyramiden combines urban exploration with a real Arctic expedition.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** fly to Svalbard Airport, Longyear (LYR), which has flights from Oslo (OSL) and Tromsø.
- **Boat tour (summer):** from about June to September, boat tours leave from Longyearbyen and take two to three hours each way, often passing a glacier on the way.
- **Snowmobile (late winter and spring):** guided snowmobile tours cross the frozen landscape to Pyramiden in a long day.
- There is no road to Pyramiden.

## Visiting

Visits are only possible on guided tours. Polar bears roam the area, so guides carry rifles and visitors may not wander off on their own. Most interiors are seen on short guided walks or from doorways. Dress for Arctic weather in every season. Tours usually allow two to three hours in the town, and overnight stays are possible at the hotel.
