# Granadilla

## Overview

Granadilla is a small walled village in the north of Extremadura in western Spain, founded in the Middle Ages on a hill above the river Alagón. In the 1950s the Spanish government built the Gabriel y Galán reservoir nearby and expropriated the village, expecting the water to flood its fields and surroundings. The inhabitants were forced to leave by the mid-1960s, although in the end the water rose around the village but never covered it. Granadilla was left almost intact: its castle, stone houses, church and complete town walls now stand on a peninsula in the reservoir. Since the 1980s a government programme has brought school groups and young volunteers to restore the houses, and the village has been declared a historic site. No one lives there permanently, but visitors can walk along the walls and climb the castle tower for views over the water.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** the nearest airport is Salamanca (SLM), about 110 km away, with few flights. Most international travellers fly to Madrid (MAD), about 280 km away.
- **Car:** about 1¼ hours from Salamanca or three hours from Madrid. There is no public transport to the village.

## Visiting

The village is open to visitors during the day; opening hours vary by season, so check before you go. The castle tower has narrow stairs. There are few services, so bring water, especially in summer.
