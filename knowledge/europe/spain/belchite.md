# Belchite

## Overview

Belchite is a town in Aragon in north-eastern Spain, about 50 km south of Zaragoza. In the summer of 1937 it was the scene of one of the fiercest battles of the Spanish Civil War, fought street by street between Republican and Nationalist forces, and much of the town was destroyed. After the war, the Franco regime decided not to rebuild the old town but to keep its ruins as a war monument, and built a new Belchite next to it, partly with the labour of political prisoners. The inhabitants lived among the ruins for years until the new town was finished, and then moved out. Today the shells of churches, a clock tower and rows of roofless houses stand as a reminder of the war and of the divisions it left behind. The old town is a protected historical site that is visited on guided tours.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Zaragoza Airport (ZAZ), about 55 km, allow 50 minutes.
- **Bus:** buses run from Zaragoza to Belchite a few times a day.

## Visiting

The old town can only be visited on guided tours, including tours at night; book ahead. Tours last about an hour and a half. The ruins are fragile, so stay with the guide. Belchite is a place of memory, and visitors are asked to behave accordingly.
