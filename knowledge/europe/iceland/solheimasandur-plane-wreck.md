# Sólheimasandur plane wreck

## Overview

On 21 November 1973 a US Navy Douglas DC-3 transport plane ran into trouble in bad weather and made an emergency landing on Sólheimasandur, a vast black sand plain on the south coast of Iceland. Everyone on board survived. The aircraft was too badly damaged to fly again, so the Navy removed what it could use and left the fuselage where it had landed. Over the years wind, sand and sea air stripped its paint, and the silver wreck lying alone on the black sand became one of Iceland's most photographed sights, appearing in music videos and advertisements. Reaching it means a walk of about 4 km each way across a flat, featureless plain from a car park beside the Ring Road. The landscape is bleak and dramatic, especially in the low light of winter or under the midnight sun of summer.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Keflavík International Airport (KEF), about 170 km, allow 2½ hours along the Ring Road (Route 1) to the car park.
- **Walk or shuttle:** from the car park, walk about 4 km each way, around 45–60 minutes, or take the shuttle bus.

## Visiting

The wreck is freely accessible; there is a parking fee. The walk is flat but exposed, and strong winds and sandstorms can arrive quickly, so check the weather and follow the marked path. Please do not climb on the wreck. Driving onto the sand is forbidden.
