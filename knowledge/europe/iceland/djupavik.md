# Djúpavík herring factory

## Overview

Djúpavík is a tiny settlement at the head of a fjord in the Strandir region of Iceland's Westfjords, one of the most remote corners of the country. In the 1930s, during Iceland's herring boom, a huge concrete factory was built here to process herring into oil and meal; when it opened in 1935 it was one of the largest concrete buildings in Iceland. Hundreds of workers came every summer, but the herring disappeared from the surrounding waters in the 1940s, and the factory closed in 1954. The village emptied, leaving the factory behind together with the rusting wreck of a ship on the beach. Since the 1980s a family has run a small hotel in the old workers' quarters and opened the factory for guided tours, exhibitions and art installations. Surrounded by steep mountains and waterfalls, Djúpavík is a quiet, atmospheric place far from the main tourist routes.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** Gjögur Airport (GJR), about 20 km away, has scheduled flights from Reykjavík a few times a week. Most international travellers arrive at Keflavík (KEF) and drive.
- **Car:** from Reykjavík, allow about five hours. The last stretch is a gravel road that is only reliably passable in summer.

## Visiting

The factory is open for guided tours in summer. The hotel offers rooms and meals and is the only service nearby. Weather and road conditions can change quickly, so check them before setting off.
