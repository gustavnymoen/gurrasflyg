# Borne Sulinowo

## Overview

Borne Sulinowo is a small town among lakes and pine forests in West Pomerania, in north-western Poland. It was built in the 1930s as a German military training area and taken over by the Soviet army after the Second World War, becoming one of the largest Soviet garrisons in Poland. For almost fifty years the town did not appear on Polish maps, and Poles were not allowed to enter. When the last Soviet soldiers left in 1992, they handed over an empty town of barracks, officers' apartment blocks, a hospital and depots. Borne Sulinowo was officially opened, new residents moved in, and today it is a quiet town of about 5,000 people. Many relics of its past remain: Soviet-era buildings, abandoned barracks in the surrounding forest, and a small museum about the town's hidden history. In summer it hosts a large rally of military vehicles.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Szczecin-Goleniów Airport (SZZ), about 140 km, allow two hours. Public transport is limited, with buses from Szczecinek.

## Visiting

The town is freely accessible. Some abandoned buildings on its outskirts are fenced off and unsafe; do not enter them. The local museum is open mainly in the summer season.
