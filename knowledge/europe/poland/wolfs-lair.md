# Wolf's Lair

## Overview

The Wolf's Lair, Wilczy Szaniec in Polish and Wolfsschanze in German, was Adolf Hitler's main military headquarters on the Eastern Front during the Second World War. Built in a forest near Kętrzyn in the Masurian lake district, then part of East Prussia, the complex contained around 200 buildings, including massive concrete bunkers with walls several metres thick, barracks, a railway station and an airfield, all camouflaged from the air. Hitler spent more than 800 days here between 1941 and 1944. It was here, on 20 July 1944, that a bomb planted by Claus von Stauffenberg exploded in a failed attempt to kill him. As the Red Army approached in January 1945, German engineers blew up the bunkers, but many were too strong to destroy completely. Today huge broken slabs of concrete lie scattered among the trees, and the site is an outdoor museum.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** the nearest airport is Olsztyn-Mazury (SZY), about 100 km away. Gdańsk (GDN), about 200 km away, has more international flights.
- **Train and bus:** trains run to Kętrzyn; from there it is about 8 km to the site by local bus or taxi.
- **Car:** about 1¼ hours from Olsztyn-Mazury Airport.

## Visiting

The site is open daily with an entry fee, and guided tours and audio guides are available. Stay on the marked paths and do not climb on the bunkers, which are unstable. In summer, bring insect repellent. Treat the site as a historical memorial.
