# Project Riese – Osówka

## Overview

Project Riese, German for "giant", was an enormous construction project started in 1943 in the Owl Mountains of Lower Silesia, in what is now south-western Poland. Beneath the mountains, Nazi Germany began digging a network of tunnels and halls whose purpose is still not fully known; theories range from an underground headquarters for Hitler to protected arms factories. The work was done by forced labourers and prisoners from a network of camps, and thousands of them died. When the war ended, the complexes were unfinished, and several of them have since been opened to visitors. Osówka is the largest of the accessible complexes, with almost 2 km of tunnels, half-finished halls and a water reservoir, together with a large concrete building above ground known as the "casino". Guided routes lead through the cold, damp galleries and explain both the engineering and the human cost of the project.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Wrocław Airport (WRO), about 90 km, allow 1½ hours.
- **Train and bus:** trains run from Wrocław to Wałbrzych in about 1¼ hours; local buses or taxis continue to Osówka.

## Visiting

Visits are on guided tours of different lengths; the longer routes include narrow passages and water. The tunnels are about 8 °C all year, so bring warm clothing and sturdy shoes. The complex was built with forced labour, and it is visited as a place of remembrance as well as engineering.
