# Wünsdorf (the 'Forbidden City')

## Overview

Wünsdorf, a small town about 40 km south of Berlin, was for most of the twentieth century one of the largest military sites in Germany. The German army built barracks and training grounds here before the First World War, and in the 1930s the Wehrmacht's high command dug a network of bunkers known as Maybach I and II, together with the Zeppelin communications centre. From the 1950s until 1994 Wünsdorf was the headquarters of the Soviet, later Russian, forces in Germany. Tens of thousands of soldiers and their families lived behind its walls, in a town that East Germans were not allowed to enter, which earned it the name "the Forbidden City". When the troops left, they left behind officers' houses, a theatre, sports halls, Lenin statues and Soviet murals. Parts of the area have since become housing and a "book town" with second-hand bookshops, while many buildings still stand empty.

## Getting there

- **Train:** regional trains run from Berlin to Wünsdorf-Waldstadt. From Berlin Brandenburg Airport (BER) allow about an hour with one change.
- **Car:** about 35 minutes from the airport.

## Visiting

Guided tours of the bunkers and the former garrison start from the visitor centre in the book town. They run regularly in season and should be booked ahead. The bunkers are cold all year, so bring a jacket. Many empty buildings are fenced off; do not enter them on your own.
