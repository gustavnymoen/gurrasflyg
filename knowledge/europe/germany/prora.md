# Prora

## Overview

Prora is a gigantic seaside complex on the island of Rügen in the Baltic Sea, built between 1936 and 1939 as a holiday resort for the Nazi organisation "Strength through Joy". Eight identical six-storey blocks stretch for about 4.5 km along the beach, designed to give 20,000 people a holiday at the same time. Construction stopped when the Second World War began, and the resort never received a single holidaymaker. After the war the Soviet army and then the East German army used parts of it as barracks, while other parts were blown up or left as empty shells. For decades the endless concrete façades stood largely abandoned and became a famous lost place. Since the 2010s several blocks have been converted into apartments, hotels and a youth hostel, but stretches of the original ruin remain. A documentation centre explains the history of the complex and the ideology behind it.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** the nearest airport is Rostock-Laage (RLG), about 160 km away, with few routes. Most international travellers fly to Berlin Brandenburg Airport (BER).
- **Train:** from Berlin, trains run to the island of Rügen in about 3½–4 hours, usually with a change in Stralsund; the line to Binz stops at Prora.
- **Car:** about two hours from Rostock-Laage, or 3½ hours from Berlin.

## Visiting

The beach promenade and the outside of the blocks are freely accessible. The documentation centre and a museum show the history of the complex and charge an entry fee. Ruined sections may be fenced off; do not enter them. In summer a visit combines easily with a day on the beach.
