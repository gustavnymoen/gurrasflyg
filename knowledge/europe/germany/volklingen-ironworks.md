# Völklingen Ironworks

## Overview

The Völklingen Ironworks in Saarland, near the French border, is the only fully preserved integrated ironworks from the great age of iron production in Western Europe and North America. Founded in the 1870s, it grew into one of Europe's leading iron producers, with six blast furnaces, a vast blowing-engine hall and a web of conveyors, bunkers and walkways. Production stopped in 1986, and only eight years later, in 1994, the site was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Today visitors can follow the route of the ore from the bunkers to the top of the blast furnaces, walk through the cavernous blower hall and explore the "Paradise", a garden where a forest has grown over the old coking plant. The ironworks is also a major venue for large art and culture exhibitions, which fill its industrial halls.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** the nearest airport is Saarbrücken (SCN), about 25 km away, with few routes. Most international travellers fly to Frankfurt (FRA) and take the train, about 2½ hours.
- **Train:** trains from Saarbrücken Hauptbahnhof reach Völklingen in about 10 minutes.
- **Walk:** the entrance is about five minutes from Völklingen station.

## Visiting

The ironworks is open daily, with an entry fee that includes the current exhibition. Allow at least half a day. The walkways and stairs up the blast furnaces are steep, and the site can be hot in summer; wear sturdy shoes and bring water.
