# Spreepark

## Overview

Spreepark opened in 1969 as the Kulturpark Plänterwald, the only permanent amusement park in East Germany, on the banks of the river Spree in south-east Berlin. After reunification a private operator took it over, renamed it Spreepark and added new rides, but visitor numbers fell and the park closed in the early 2000s, deep in debt. For almost twenty years its rusting roller coaster, toppled fibreglass dinosaurs, swan-shaped boats and large Ferris wheel slowly disappeared into the undergrowth, and the park became a magnet for urban explorers, film crews and photographers. The city of Berlin bought the site back in 2014 and is turning it into a new kind of park that combines art, nature and some of the old attractions, including the restored Ferris wheel. The reopening is planned for spring 2027, which will end the park's long life as one of Berlin's best-known abandoned places.

## Getting there

- **Train and walk:** from Berlin Brandenburg Airport (BER), take the S-Bahn to Plänterwald or Treptower Park, about 30 minutes, then walk 10–15 minutes through the Plänterwald forest.
- **Taxi:** about 25 minutes from the airport.

## Visiting

The park is closed while it is being rebuilt. Check the park's official channels for any guided previews or events before the planned reopening in spring 2027. Do not climb the fences: the site is an active construction area.
