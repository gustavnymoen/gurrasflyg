# Beelitz-Heilstätten

## Overview

Beelitz-Heilstätten is a huge hospital complex in the pine forests south-west of Berlin, built from the 1890s as a sanatorium for tuberculosis patients from the city's working class. Its brick pavilions, spread across a large park, held well over a thousand beds. During the First World War it served as a military hospital, where a young Adolf Hitler was treated in 1916. From 1945 until 1994 the Soviet army ran it as one of its largest military hospitals outside the Soviet Union. After the soldiers left, most of the buildings stood empty, and their crumbling halls, operating theatres and overgrown roofs made Beelitz one of Germany's most photographed lost places. Parts of the site have since been restored as housing and clinics, while others remain ruins. A treetop walkway now lets visitors look down on the decaying roofs, and guided tours open some of the empty buildings.

## Getting there

- **Train:** from Berlin Brandenburg Airport (BER), regional trains reach Beelitz-Heilstätten station, right next to the site, in about 1–1½ hours with one change.
- **Car:** about 45–60 minutes from the airport.

## Visiting

The treetop walkway and the ruins around it are open to visitors with an entry fee. Guided tours take groups inside some of the empty buildings and should be booked ahead. Entering the ruins on your own is forbidden and dangerous: much of the site is private property and is patrolled.
