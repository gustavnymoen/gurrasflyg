# Teufelsberg listening station

## Overview

Teufelsberg is an artificial hill in Berlin's Grunewald forest, piled up after the Second World War from the rubble of the bombed city. On its summit stand the remains of a Cold War listening station, run by the United States and the United Kingdom to intercept radio traffic from East Germany and the wider Eastern Bloc. Its white radomes, the domes that once sheltered the antennas, can still be seen from across western Berlin. When the Allies left in the early 1990s, investors planned hotels and flats on the hill, but the projects failed and the station was left to decay. Today the site is privately run and open to visitors for an entry fee. Its walls and towers carry large-scale graffiti and street art, making it one of Berlin's best-known urban exploration sites. From the top of the main tower there are wide views over the forest and the city.

## Getting there

- **Train and walk:** from Berlin Brandenburg Airport (BER), take a regional train or S-Bahn into the city and change to the S-Bahn towards Spandau, getting off at Heerstraße. From there it is a 25–30 minute walk uphill through the forest. Allow about 1¼ hours in total.
- **Car or taxi:** about 45 minutes from the airport. Park at the edge of the forest and walk the last part.

## Visiting

The station is open to visitors most days, with an entry fee, and guided tours explain its history. The site is uneven and partly unfinished: wear sturdy shoes and stay within the marked areas. The top of the radome tower is exposed and can be windy and cold. Allow two to three hours.
