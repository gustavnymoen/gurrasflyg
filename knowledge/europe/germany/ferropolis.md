# Ferropolis

## Overview

Ferropolis, "the city of iron", lies on a peninsula in a lake that was once the Golpa-Nord open-cast lignite mine near Dessau in Saxony-Anhalt. When mining ended in the early 1990s, five of the giant machines that had dug the coal were saved from the scrapyard and arranged in a semicircle, like the stage of an arena. The largest are more than 30 metres tall and over 100 metres long, and together they weigh thousands of tonnes. The old mine has since been flooded to form the Gremminer See, and the steel giants now tower over a grassy amphitheatre used for music festivals and concerts. Visitors can climb onto some of the excavators, learn how lignite mining reshaped the landscape of eastern Germany, and walk or cycle around the lake.

## Getting there

- **Train and taxi:** from Leipzig/Halle Airport (LEJ), trains reach Gräfenhainichen via Leipzig or Bitterfeld in about 1–1½ hours. From the station, take a taxi or walk about 30 minutes.
- **Car:** about 45 minutes from the airport.

## Visiting

Ferropolis is open to visitors for most of the year, with an entry fee; check opening times, especially in winter. During large festivals the site is closed to day visitors. Guided tours, including climbing tours on the excavators, are offered in season.
