# Zollverein Coal Mine

## Overview

The Zollverein Coal Mine Industrial Complex in Essen was once one of the largest coal mines in Europe. Its Shaft XII, opened in 1932, was designed in a clean, Bauhaus-inspired style by the architects Fritz Schupp and Martin Kremmer and became known as "the most beautiful coal mine in the world". The mine closed in 1986 and the coking plant next to it in 1993, ending more than 130 years of mining on the site. Instead of being demolished, the buildings were preserved, and in 2001 Zollverein was added to the UNESCO World Heritage list. Today the huge brick halls, conveyor bridges and coking ovens house the Ruhr Museum, design exhibitions, restaurants and event spaces, while the grounds are open to walk around freely. Zollverein has become the symbol of the Ruhr region's change from heavy industry to culture.

## Getting there

- **Train and tram:** from Düsseldorf Airport (DUS), take a train to Essen Hauptbahnhof, about 25 minutes, then tram 107 to Zollverein, about 15 minutes.
- **Car:** about 30 minutes from the airport.

## Visiting

The grounds are open all day and free to walk around. The museums and guided tours of the coal washery and coking plant charge entry fees. In summer there is an open-air swimming pool at the coking plant, and in winter an ice rink. Allow at least half a day.
