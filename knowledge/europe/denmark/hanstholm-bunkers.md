# Hanstholm bunkers

## Overview

Hanstholm, on the windswept north-west coast of Jutland in Denmark, was one of the most heavily fortified places in northern Europe during the Second World War. The German army built a fortress here as part of the Atlantic Wall, with four giant 38 cm guns that, together with a battery on the Norwegian side, could close the Skagerrak strait to Allied ships. Hundreds of bunkers, gun positions and tunnels were built in the dunes, and local inhabitants had to leave the area. After the war the guns were scrapped, but the concrete remained. Today the Museumscenter Hanstholm occupies one of the gun bunkers, with an underground tour and a narrow-gauge ammunition railway, while many other bunkers lie half buried in the sand along the coast. The surrounding area is part of Thy National Park.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Aalborg Airport (AAL), about 95 km, allow 1½ hours.
- **Train and bus:** trains run from Aalborg to Thisted; from there, a bus continues to Hanstholm.

## Visiting

The museum charges an entry fee and has seasonal opening hours. Many bunkers in the dunes can be seen freely; some are open, others sealed. Do not enter unstable bunkers, and keep to the paths to protect the dunes.
