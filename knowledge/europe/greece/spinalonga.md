# Spinalonga

## Overview

Spinalonga is a small rocky island in the Gulf of Elounda on the north-east coast of Crete. The Venetians built a powerful sea fortress there in the sixteenth century to protect the harbour, and it held out against the Ottomans long after the rest of Crete had fallen. In 1903 the island became a colony for people with leprosy, who were sent there from across Crete and later from all of Greece. The patients lived in the old houses inside the walls, with a church, shops and a hospital, cut off from the world, until effective treatment arrived and the colony closed in 1957, making it one of the last leper colonies in Europe. The abandoned island became widely known through Victoria Hislop's novel The Island. Today visitors walk through the fortress gate and along the main street, past ruined houses, the disinfection building and the cemetery.

## Getting there

- **Bus or car:** from Heraklion International Airport (HER), about 65 km, travel to Elounda or Plaka, around an hour by car.
- **Ferry:** frequent boats cross to the island from Plaka in about 10 minutes and from Elounda in about 20 minutes. Excursion boats also run from Agios Nikolaos.

## Visiting

The island is open daily with an entry fee. There is little shade, so bring water and sun protection in summer. Spinalonga was home to people who suffered long isolation; please visit with respect.
