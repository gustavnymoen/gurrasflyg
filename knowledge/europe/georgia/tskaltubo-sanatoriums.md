# Tskaltubo sanatoriums

## Overview

Tskaltubo is a spa town in western Georgia, close to the city of Kutaisi, known for its warm, mildly radioactive mineral springs. In Soviet times it was one of the most popular health resorts in the Soviet Union, with direct trains from Moscow and around twenty sanatoriums built in a grand neoclassical style around a large park, together with ornate bathhouses where patients took the waters. Joseph Stalin had his own private bath here. After the collapse of the Soviet Union the visitors disappeared, and from the 1990s many sanatoriums housed people displaced by the war in Abkhazia, some of whom still live there. Others were left to decay, their columned halls, mosaics and staircases slowly taken over by plants. In recent years some buildings have been restored as hotels, and the town is trying to revive its spa tradition, but many of the old sanatoriums still stand empty.

## Getting there

- **Taxi:** from Kutaisi International Airport (KUT), about 15 km, allow 20 minutes.
- **Bus:** minibuses (marshrutkas) run between Kutaisi and Tskaltubo.

## Visiting

Many sanatoriums can be seen from the street and the park. Some are inhabited, so respect residents' privacy and ask before entering; others are guarded or fenced, and floors and stairs in empty buildings can be unsafe. Some bathhouses still operate, and you can take a bath in the mineral water.
