# Petrova Gora Memorial

## Overview

The Petrova Gora Memorial stands on the summit of Petrova Gora, a forested hill in central Croatia near the border with Bosnia and Herzegovina. It commemorates the people of the Kordun and Banija regions who died in the Second World War, many of them killed in 1942 when German and Ustaša forces tried to destroy the Partisan hospital and the civilians hiding in these woods. Designed by the sculptor Vojin Bakić and completed in 1981, the building is a tall, curving concrete structure that was clad in shining stainless steel, rising above the trees like an abstract sculpture. Inside were a museum, a library and a viewing platform. After the wars of the 1990s it was abandoned, and much of the steel cladding was stripped and sold, leaving the concrete exposed. Today it stands empty and weathered, one of the most powerful and melancholy of the Yugoslav memorials.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Zagreb Airport (ZAG), about 75 km, allow 1½ hours. The last part is a forest road. There is no public transport.

## Visiting

The grounds around the memorial are freely accessible, but the building is closed and unsafe; do not go inside, as debris can fall. The forest roads can be in poor condition, especially in winter.
