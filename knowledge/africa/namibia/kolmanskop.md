# Kolmanskop

## Overview

Kolmanskop is a ghost town in the Namib Desert, about 10 km inland from the port of Lüderitz in southern Namibia. In 1908, when Namibia was the German colony of South West Africa, a railway worker named Zacharias Lewala found a diamond in the sand here and showed it to his supervisor. A diamond rush followed, and the Germans built a prosperous town in the middle of the desert, with large stone villas, a ballroom, a theatre, a bowling alley, a school and a hospital with one of the first X-ray machines in the southern hemisphere. Fresh water had to be brought by train, and ice was made in the town's own factory. After the First World War, diamond production fell and richer deposits were found further south, so people gradually left, and the town was abandoned by the 1950s. Since then the desert has moved in, and today rooms are knee-deep in sand.

## Getting there

- **Car:** from Lüderitz Airport (LUD), about 10 km, the drive takes 15 minutes.
- **Plane:** Lüderitz has few flights; many visitors drive from Hosea Kutako International Airport (WDH) near Windhoek, about 700 km, a long day's drive.

## Visiting

Kolmanskop lies in a restricted diamond area and can only be visited on guided tours, which leave in the morning; buy a permit at the gate. Photographers can buy a special permit for longer visits. Bring water and sun protection. Wind can blow sand hard in the afternoon.
