# Elizabeth Bay

## Overview

Elizabeth Bay is an abandoned diamond-mining town on the Atlantic coast of southern Namibia, about 25 km south of Lüderitz. It lies inside the Sperrgebiet, the "forbidden area", a large region that was closed to the public for most of the twentieth century to protect the diamond fields. The town was built in the mid-1920s, when rich diamond deposits were found along this part of the coast, and the mining company built houses, a hospital, workshops and a power station for its workers and their families. The deposits ran out quickly, and the town was abandoned by around 1950. Because Elizabeth Bay lies right on the coast, the strong south winds have blasted the buildings with sand, and many walls have been worn down or have collapsed. Fewer people visit than nearby Kolmanskop, which makes it feel even lonelier.

## Getting there

- **Plane:** Lüderitz Airport (LUD) is about 25 km away; Hosea Kutako International Airport (WDH) near Windhoek is the main international airport.
- **4x4 tour:** licensed tour operators in Lüderitz drive visitors into the restricted area.

## Visiting

Elizabeth Bay can only be visited on licensed tours with a permit. Book a few days ahead and bring your passport, as the operator needs your details to apply. Tours often combine Elizabeth Bay with Kolmanskop. Expect strong wind and blowing sand.
